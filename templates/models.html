Why {{ card.production_model }} is in production

The candidates are separated by a hair on discrimination — the gap between the best and worst AUC here is smaller than the noise you would get from reshuffling the train/test split. What is not close is explainability: PS #4 requires a defensible reason for every individual decision, and the Random Forest is the only candidate whose single-prediction output decomposes exactly into per-feature contributions. Trading a fraction of a point of AUC for an explanation you can put in front of a rejected applicant is the right trade for lending.

Calibration

Does a predicted 70% actually repay 70% of the time?

Points on the dashed line mean the probabilities can be trusted as probabilities — which is what lets a lender price risk rather than merely rank it. Ranking quality (AUC) and calibration are different properties, and a model can have one without the other.

Data-consistency screen

A separate unsupervised model (Isolation Forest) looks for applications whose numbers don't hang together the way genuine ones do — a large claimed inflow on a handful of transactions, filings inconsistent with turnover. It flags roughly {{ (anomaly_rate * 100)|round(0)|int }}% of files for manual verification. It never blocks a decision on its own; it routes the file to a human, which is how a real lender treats a soft fraud signal.

Where the confidence band comes from

Each applicant's band is the wider of two independent doubts: how much of their file we actually have, and how much the forest's own trees disagree about them. The second is measured as the standard error of the ensemble's prediction — where trees diverge, the model is extrapolating rather than recognising, and the band widens to say so.
