About

No file, no loan

Why a viable business gets rejected by a system that never actually looked at it.

A large share of Indian borrowers and micro-enterprises have little or no formal credit history. A bureau score either doesn't exist for them or is meaningless — so lenders respond the only way an opaque system allows: reject outright, or price the risk crudely with a blanket high rate.

Either way, working capital stays out of reach of businesses that are, in reality, perfectly capable of repaying. The information to prove it exists — it's just not in the bureau.

What we score instead

The data these borrowers already generate every day:

  • Digital payment flows — how much moves through their account, whether it's growing, how steady it is, how often payments bounce
  • GST & invoice history — filing discipline and overdue receivables
  • Utility & rent payments — the most universal proof of reliability there is
  • Supplier relationships — concentration risk and repeat-business stability
  • Business vintage — how far the track record actually goes back

Gender, geography and business type are deliberately kept out of the model. They appear only in the fairness audit, to verify the model isn't systematically harsher on any group — never as an input to someone's score.

Accurate isn't enough

A model that scores well on average but is opaque, unfair, or careless about affordability isn't good enough for this problem — it just automates the same exclusion faster. So CredVeda also:

  • Explains every score in plain language, in both a lender view and an applicant view
  • Returns a confidence band when the data is thin, instead of faking precision
  • Audits itself across geography, gender and business type, and publishes the gaps
  • Refuses approvals that would create an unsustainable repayment burden
  • Shows every borrower a concrete six-month path to a better score

A note on the data

This build runs on a large, realistic synthetic population of individuals and small businesses. Real UPI/Account Aggregator feeds, the GST portal and credit bureaus all require regulated data-sharing partnerships that aren't available for a hackathon build. Everything downstream — the feature schema, model, explainability, fairness audit and guardrails — is written against a fixed schema, so swapping in real connectors is a data-layer change, not a rebuild.
