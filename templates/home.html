The data a bureau never sees

Five families of alternative signal, engineered into 18 model features — no credit history required.

Digital payment flows

Inflow scale, 6-month growth, month-to-month volatility, transaction count, bounce rate and how much of their activity is digitally traceable at all.

GST & invoice history

Registration status, how reliably returns are filed on time, and what share of outstanding invoices have gone overdue.

Utility & rent payments

The most universal signal of all — does this person pay their bills on time, month after month, when nobody is checking their credit?

Supplier relationships

Concentration risk across suppliers and buyers, and how much of the book is repeat business versus one-off trade.

Business vintage

How far back the operating or income history actually goes — the single strongest structural signal for a thin-file borrower.

Affordability

Existing plus proposed EMI against estimated net cash flow, so a good score can never quietly push through an unaffordable loan.

Built to be defensible, not just accurate

A score you can't explain, can't audit, and can't act on is a liability. Here's what ships with every decision.

Plain-language reason codes

Explainable modelling · Reason codes

Every prediction is decomposed feature-by-feature into an exact additive explanation, then mapped to standard codes (RC01–RC14) with sentences an applicant can actually read — so a rejection can be justified to the person receiving it, not just logged.

Honest uncertainty

Thin-file handling

When the data is sparse, CredVeda refuses to fake precision. It reports a confidence band — “somewhere between 520 and 640” — instead of a confident-looking number that the evidence doesn't support.

Fairness under audit

Fairness testing

Gender, geography and business type are withheld from the model entirely, then used after the fact to test outcomes against the four-fifths rule — and we publish where the model comes out harsher, rather than burying it in an average.

A way forward

Borrower improvement path

Rejected applicants get the two or three behaviours that would most improve their score over six months — each one simulated against the real model, not pulled from a list of generic tips.
