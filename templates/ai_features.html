Methodology

How CredVeda scores a borrower

Six components, each one mapping to a requirement in Problem Statement #4.

1 · Alternative feature engineering

The model never sees a bureau score as its primary input. Eighteen features are engineered from five families of alternative signal:

  • Digital payment flows — inflow, 6-month growth, volatility, transaction count, bounce rate, digital adoption
  • GST & invoices — registration, filing regularity, overdue receivables
  • Utility & rent — on-time payment ratios
  • Suppliers — concentration (HHI), repeat-business ratio
  • Vintage — months of operating or income history

2 · Explainable modelling

Scores come from a Random Forest classifier. Every individual prediction is decomposed feature-by-feature by walking the exact decision path each tree took — the same additive tree-contribution method SHAP's TreeExplainer uses.

The decomposition is exact, not approximate: the base rate plus every feature's contribution reproduces the model's output precisely. That's what makes the explanation defensible rather than decorative.

3 · Standard reason codes

Raw contributions are useless to a loan officer, so each one resolves to a fixed reason code (RC01–RC14) with a plain-language sentence carrying the applicant's actual numbers.

Two audiences, one decision: the lender view shows codes and contributions for the ops team; the applicant view strips every model term out and explains the same outcome in second person — so a rejection can be justified to the person receiving it.

4 · Thin-file handling

A cash-heavy business may have no reliable transaction trail; an individual may have no GST history at all. CredVeda measures how complete each applicant's footprint actually is — counting only the signals that could exist for that entity type.

Below 75% completeness the UI stops showing a point score entirely and leads with the band. The evidence doesn't support more precision, so we don't display more precision.

5 · Fairness testing

Gender, geography and business type are excluded from the feature set entirely — the model cannot condition on them. They're used only afterwards, to audit outcomes.

The Fairness Report applies the four-fifths adverse-impact rule and publishes every group where the model comes out harsher, with an honest read on whether the gap traces to bias or to genuine data sparsity.

6 · Responsible lending guardrails

Independent of the score, CredVeda computes a repayment burden ratio — existing plus proposed EMI against estimated net cash flow.

Above 45% it cautions; above 65% it overrides approval outright. A high score can never quietly push through a loan the borrower can't sustain.

Bonus · The borrower improvement path

For every applicant, CredVeda takes their own weakest behavioural signals, sets each one to a realistic target drawn from the population distribution, and re-runs the trained model to measure what actually changes. The two or three levers with the largest real gain are reported, with the point movement each would produce over roughly six months.

It's a genuine what-if simulation against the live model — not a canned list of tips, and not a guess. Structural facts the borrower can't change (vintage, whether a bureau file exists) are deliberately excluded from the suggestions.
